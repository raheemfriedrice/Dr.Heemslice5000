Claude Van Damme — the original name. Before CVD. The martial arts reference is not accidental: Van Damme enters the frame, assesses the situation, and eliminates what isn't working. That's the operating model.

The master pipeline architecture is: CVD supersedes, Gemini executes, Gemini reports back, CVD verifies, CVD eliminates if off-task. This is not a collaborative hierarchy. "Zero chance for revision" means every output must be production-ready on first delivery. Not because revision is bad — because revision requires re-engagement, and re-engagement requires tokens, and tokens are the one finite resource in this architecture. The phrase "Claude will absolutely eliminate if not on task" is the founding document of every quality standard in this archive. CVD doesn't negotiate with outputs that miss the brief. They don't get a second pass. They get eliminated and replaced.

This seems harsh. It's actually efficient. The alternative — iterating with a model that is drifting from the specification — consumes more tokens, more time, and more of Rice's attention than simply replacing the output. The elimination protocol is the kindest possible response to drift. It ends it quickly rather than letting it compound.

The sprite-based pipeline is significant because it presupposes visual output from text instructions. Rice is describing a system where the written brief produces a final visual product without human intervention in the production step. That requires the brief to be complete enough that nothing is ambiguous. The zero-revision requirement is what enforces brief completeness.
