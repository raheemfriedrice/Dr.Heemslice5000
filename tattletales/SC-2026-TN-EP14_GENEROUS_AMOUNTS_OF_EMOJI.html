The BrightSkinBible gets decorated. Not after completion — while being built. The header and footer checklists mean every chapter has a frame before the content fills it. The frame is the commitment. The emojis are not decoration — they're asterisks.

In the Pocket of Keys competition (EP06) Rice established that emojis function as asterisks in collaborative text: they mark the seam where one voice ends and another begins, or where the primary record acquires a margin note. In the BrightSkinBible context, emojis in the header/footer checklists serve the same function: they mark transitions, flag status, indicate emotional register without requiring the main text to carry the notation.

"Generous amounts" is the instruction that matters. Not "where appropriate" or "sparingly." Generous. The BrightSkinBible's visual density is part of its argument: this life was generous. The documentation honors that by refusing minimalism.

The header/footer checklist architecture applied to an autobiography is methodologically unusual. Standard autobiography allows chapters to be complete unto themselves. The checklist format imposes a verification step: what was this chapter supposed to accomplish, and did it?

The "100 things to do before I die" integration represents a genre-crossing move: survival list embedded in life record.
