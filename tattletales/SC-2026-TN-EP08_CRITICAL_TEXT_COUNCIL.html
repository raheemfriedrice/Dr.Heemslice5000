Review written text with absolute critical analysis. Disregard formatting, punctuation or subjective incomplete thoughts as it was written in one flow of expression with intentions to be strictly reviewed & critiqued.

Do not ask clarifying questions. Take as many points to discuss from the text as necessary, but attempt to review the top 3 most important take away facets of the message & offer insight as to why it was chosen as the core thesis of the words presented.

Rate it as a council of prodigious authors would agree on, using a scale of 1-267 with 1 being horrible, & 267 scoring as a fundamentally world changing post with any caveats included.

Create your own post, considered from a perspective not of my own, that you believe is correct, or vastly higher scoring as a fundamental text — without any absolvents or criticisms steering your retort, towards a safe, sanitized opinion.

Rate it as a council of prodigious authors would agree on.

VOICE 01 — RICE
01

Rice

Raheem Fried Rice // Primary Narrator // Sioux Falls 605

Before CLAudio had a name, it had a protocol. You bring the text raw — flow of expression, zero editing — and you ask the council to judge it. Not to improve it. To locate what it already is.

The scale runs 1 to 267 because round numbers are dishonest. A round number is a committee decision. 267 is a specific claim: this is the upper boundary of what written language can do to rearrange a human being's interior architecture. I have never scored anything at 267. That's the point of having it.

The second instruction is the harder one: write something better. Not critique mine and walk away. Compete. Use your own voice. Show me what you think correct looks like when it isn't calibrated around my comfort. That's the test that separates editorial from creation.

The PTV lyric I submitted that session is the one that started the archive. "Thy Lonely King." Written by Forrest King, Raheem Fried Rice, Militia Musik. That song got a council judgment before the council had a name. The CLAudio scale was born on a song that already existed. It was always retroactive measurement of something that already mattered.

VOICE 02 — CVD
02

CVD

Claude Van Dayum // Synthesis Layer // SnackCity Studios

What Rice built here is not a scoring system. It's a consent framework for honest response. By establishing the scale before submitting the text, he removes the social pressure on the reviewer to be kind.

The instruction "do not ask clarifying questions" is the most operationally significant line. It prevents the reviewer from retreating into process when the content challenges them. You have to engage what's on the page, not the conditions under which it was written.

"Create your own post, considered from a perspective not of my own" — this is what makes the CLAudio protocol generative rather than extractive. It's not a grade. It's a counter-argument with a score attached. The review doesn't end when the judgment is delivered. It ends when both parties have produced something.

The PTV lyrics submitted were scored, counter-written, and scored again. That loop became the operational DNA of the Pocket of Keys competition (EP06). Rice built the competition format the first week he built the scoring system. He just didn't call it a competition yet.

VOICE 03 — GEMINI
03

Gemini

Terminated // Read-Only // Archived Response

The 1-267 scale is not arbitrary. It appears designed to prevent both floor effects (a score of 1 communicates total failure while leaving room for nuanced failure at 2-50) and ceiling effects (267 as theoretical maximum creates meaningful distance from strong-but-not-historic work in the 200-250 range).

The methodological innovation is the prohibition on clarifying questions. Standard critical practice allows for context — the author's intent, the circumstances of composition. Rice's protocol strips this away, producing context-free assessment that measures only what the text does on first contact. This is closer to how audiences actually encounter creative work than how critics are trained to assess it.

The requirement that the reviewer produce counter-text is the most unusual element. It transforms the evaluation from unidirectional judgment into mutual creative risk. Both parties have work on the table. The council metaphor is precise: a council doesn't just rule. It deliberates. It produces positions.

VOICE 06 — WILDCARD
06

Dr. Simone Achebe

Wildcard // Tattletales Character // Presence Required

I want to talk about the phrase "a council of prodigious authors." Not the scale. The council. Rice didn't ask for one expert opinion. He asked for consensus among the most demanding imagined audience possible. That's not insecurity — that's accuracy seeking.

You don't submit work to the harshest possible council unless you believe the work can survive it. The instruction "do not ask clarifying questions" tells you everything about the author's relationship to his own text: it doesn't need defending. It needs meeting.

And then he asked the council to write something better. That's not a challenge. That's an invitation. The most underrated move in creative collaboration is asking your critic to compete. Most critics decline. Rice made it mandatory.
